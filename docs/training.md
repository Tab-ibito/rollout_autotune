# Offline Training and Surrogate Physics

`training/train.py` pretrains a step-fused causal Decision Importance Transformer (DIT) from suboptimal multi-policy trajectories generated by `training/simulator.py` (optionally combined with real hardware JSONL logs).

## Closed-loop surrogate (`training/simulator.py`)

Each synthetic world samples parameters across three axes:

- **Policy model tiers**: Lightweight MLP/ACT policies (`0.6–4s` cold load, `0.8–2.5 GB` VRAM, fast inference), medium models, and 7B+ VLA models (`14–40s` cold load, `5.5–11 GB` VRAM).
- **GPU envelopes**: `16–26 GB`, `30–50 GB`, and `58–82 GB` VRAM caps paired with diverse throughput ceilings.
- **Environment & device contention**: Per-worker CPU throughput with knee throttling (`cpu_knee`), optional GPU background rendering load (`egl_penalty`, modeling MuJoCo EGL offscreen rendering that raises `slowdown > 1.2` even at `g = 1`), and shard-to-shard step-rate variance (`shard_sigma`).

Aggregate GPU inference throughput follows the parallel service identity `R_gpu(k, g) = min(gpu_cap, g * R_1 / S(k, g))`. Even when `S(k, g)` reaches `1.6–2.2`, adding replicas increases throughput whenever `g / S(k, g)` grows. Under default hot-scaling physics, replica scale-up runs asynchronously in background while imposing a `4%–11%` transient PCIe/HBM copy dip during the overlap interval.

## Two-stage DIT objective (`training/train.py`)

1. **Stage 1 — Joint Critic (`Q`, `V`, `μ`)**:
   - `Q(H_t, a)` fits **standard MSE** against TD targets, yielding risk-neutral expected returns over latent startup costs (`load_s`) and shard fluctuations without Jensen optimism bias.
   - `V(H_t)` fits an **asymmetric `τ = 0.62` expectile loss** against detached `Q(H_t, a_t)`, performing in-sample multi-step trajectory stitching across mixed behavioral policies (multi-scale threshold explorers, sticky random walks, and noisy hill-climbers).
   - `μ(a | H_t)` fits masked cross-entropy over behavioral actions to estimate action propensities.
2. **Stage 2 — Debiased Weighted MLE Actor (`π`)**:
   - Expected advantage `A_t = Q(H_t, a_t) - V(H_t)` is normalized by batch standard deviation (floored at `0.05`).
   - Actor cross-entropy is weighted by `μ(a_t | H_t)^(-0.55) * clamp(exp(norm_adv / 0.4), 0.02, 20.0)`. Inverse-propensity weighting cancels behavioral action-frequency bias (preventing historical `g − 1` habits from dominating), while keeping negative-advantage switches uncensored ensures heavy startup costs suppress unprofitable switches.
